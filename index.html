01

Purpose

Rexscot is a cybersecurity consulting firm that provides penetration testing, vulnerability assessment, and security consulting services to organisations across India and internationally. We take the security of our own systems and public-facing assets seriously.

This Vulnerability Disclosure Policy (VDP) establishes a clear and transparent process for security researchers, users, customers, and members of the broader security community to responsibly report suspected vulnerabilities affecting Rexscot's own published in-scope assets.

Rexscot believes in the value of responsible security research and recognises that external researchers may sometimes identify potential security issues before internal teams.

This policy applies exclusively to Rexscot's own published in-scope assets. It does not cover client systems, client infrastructure, or third-party services. Separate engagement documentation governs all client security testing.

02

Scope

2A — In-Scope Assets

The following Rexscot-operated assets are within the scope of this policy. Researchers should verify current scope before conducting any testing.

Asset TypeAsset / IdentifierNotes
Primary Websitehttps://rexscot.comPublic-facing site

Scope may be updated periodically. Always verify current in-scope assets before testing. If uncertain, contact all@rexscot.com before conducting any testing.

2B — Out-of-Scope Assets

  • Infrastructure not listed as in-scope above
  • Third-party services, SaaS providers, and cloud platforms used by Rexscot
  • Internet infrastructure not operated by Rexscot
  • Systems belonging to Rexscot's clients or customers
  • Employee personal accounts and personal devices
  • Physical premises and physical security
  • Social engineering targets including employees, contractors, or partners

2C — Third-Party Systems

Rexscot relies on various third-party providers. These systems are not within scope. Researchers who identify a vulnerability in a third-party system that affects Rexscot may report it to the relevant vendor directly and notify Rexscot separately at all@rexscot.com.

2D — Client and Customer Systems

Important: Rexscot provides security testing services to client organisations. A researcher must not assume that any client domain, application, server, IP address, API, or cloud account is authorised for testing merely because Rexscot works with that organisation. Client systems require separate, written authorisation. Testing client systems without authorisation may constitute a criminal offence.

05

Safe Reporting Practices

Researchers are expected to conduct vulnerability discovery responsibly and minimise potential harm to Rexscot systems, data, and users.

  • Minimise access to data beyond what is strictly necessary to confirm the vulnerability
  • Avoid accessing, modifying, deleting, or exfiltrating user data or production data
  • Stop testing immediately once sufficient evidence has been gathered
  • Avoid disrupting services, creating outages, or degrading system performance
  • Avoid creating or deploying persistence mechanisms, backdoors, or malware
  • Protect any sensitive information unintentionally accessed during research
  • Promptly notify Rexscot if sensitive or personal information was unexpectedly encountered
  • Securely delete any sensitive data collected once it is no longer needed for the report

06

Permitted Security Research

Within the published scope defined in Section 2A, the following types of low-impact research activities may be acceptable:

  • Passive reconnaissance of publicly accessible information
  • Identification of publicly visible security misconfigurations
  • Low-impact vulnerability verification using controlled proof-of-concept
  • Authentication and authorisation testing strictly within published scope
  • Non-destructive validation of reported issues
  • Automated scanning of limited scope with minimal traffic

Submitting a vulnerability report does NOT authorise broader security testing. Research not covered by published scope requires separate written authorisation from Rexscot.

08

Test Data and Account Safety

  • Use researcher-owned or designated test accounts — do not use real user accounts without explicit permission
  • Do not submit real production credentials, passwords, API keys, or private keys in reports
  • Do not retain or publish authentication tokens or session cookies discovered during research
  • Avoid collecting personal information about Rexscot employees, clients, or users
  • Do not access client data beyond what is necessary to confirm the vulnerability
  • If real credentials are inadvertently discovered, notify Rexscot immediately and do not use or retain them

09

Rexscot uses third-party services as part of its operations. Third-party infrastructure is not automatically within scope.

Researchers who identify a vulnerability involving a third-party provider should report directly to the relevant vendor's own disclosure programme and may notify Rexscot separately where the issue has direct implications for Rexscot's security posture. Do not exploit or pivot through third-party vulnerabilities to attack Rexscot or its clients.

This section is critical. Rexscot provides penetration testing and security consulting to client organisations. As a result, Rexscot may be associated with a wide range of client domains, applications, and infrastructure.

A vulnerability researcher must not assume that any of the following are authorised for testing under this policy:

  • Client or customer servers, IP ranges, or cloud accounts

Any security testing of client systems requires a separate, written Authorisation to Test signed by the relevant client, together with a formal Rexscot engagement agreement. Submitting a contact form or vulnerability report to Rexscot does not constitute client authorisation.

14

Responsible Disclosure

Rexscot requests that researchers allow reasonable time to investigate, validate, and remediate reported vulnerabilities before any public disclosure.

Researchers are encouraged to coordinate disclosure timing with Rexscot. Premature public disclosure of an unpatched vulnerability may harm Rexscot's clients, users, and security posture.

Where coordinated disclosure is agreed, Rexscot may consider acknowledging the researcher's contribution, subject to operational and legal considerations. Rexscot does not guarantee public credit, CVE assignment, hall-of-fame listing, or blog recognition.

Rexscot does not impose a mandatory embargo period. Researchers are asked to act in good faith and contact all@rexscot.com to discuss disclosure timing.

15

Public Disclosure

Following remediation, Rexscot may consider coordinated public disclosure where appropriate and where the researcher has acted in accordance with this policy.

Rexscot does not promise: public credit or attribution, CVE ID assignment, hall-of-fame listings, blog posts about specific reports, or monetary compensation of any kind.

This policy is not a bug-bounty programme. No financial rewards are offered or implied.

16

Duplicate and Out-of-Scope Reports

Duplicates

Multiple researchers may independently identify the same underlying vulnerability. Rexscot may classify subsequent reports as duplicates and will acknowledge them where practical.

Out-of-Scope / Invalid

Reports may be classified as out-of-scope or invalid where they involve assets not in published scope, third-party or client systems, theoretical issues without reproducible evidence, automated scanner output without manual validation, or informational observations without security impact.

17

Safe Harbor

Legal Review Required
This section reflects Rexscot's intended approach and has not been reviewed by qualified legal counsel. It does not constitute a legal guarantee or binding commitment. Applicable Indian and international law may still apply.

Rexscot does not intend to pursue legal action against researchers who:

  • Conduct research within published in-scope assets (Section 2A)
  • Comply with all restrictions in this policy, particularly Section 7 (Prohibited Activities)
  • Report vulnerabilities promptly and in good faith through the designated reporting channel
  • Minimise harm in accordance with Section 5 (Safe Reporting Practices)
  • Do not exploit vulnerabilities beyond confirming the finding
  • Do not publicly disclose before Rexscot has had reasonable time to investigate

This section does not: authorise testing outside published scope, override applicable law, waive the legal rights of third parties including Rexscot's clients, create a contractual relationship, or constitute a legal defence against criminal or civil proceedings.

18

Data Confidentiality

Legal Review Required
Data handling obligations under applicable privacy law (including the DPDP Act) should be reviewed by qualified legal counsel.

Researchers who submit reports are expected to:

  • Minimise access to sensitive data beyond confirming the vulnerability
  • Not publish, share, or distribute any confidential information encountered during research
  • Securely delete any sensitive data collected as soon as it is no longer needed for the report
  • Notify Rexscot if sensitive personal data, client data, or credentials were unintentionally accessed

Rexscot will treat vulnerability reports submitted in good faith with appropriate confidentiality. Rexscot does not guarantee absolute confidentiality in all circumstances, including where legal or regulatory obligations may require disclosure.

19

AI-Assisted Reporting and Research

Rexscot acknowledges that researchers may use AI-assisted tools and workflows. The following principles apply:

  • AI-generated reports may be submitted; the researcher takes full responsibility for accuracy and completeness
  • Unsupported AI-generated claims without reproducible evidence may require additional supporting information
  • Researchers must not submit Rexscot's confidential information to public AI systems to generate a report
  • Fabricated or misleading AI-generated evidence is not acceptable and may result in the report being dismissed
  • Researchers remain personally responsible for the accuracy, legality, and ethics of their research regardless of AI tool usage

20

Report Quality

High-quality reports enable faster triage and remediation. A useful report includes:

  • A clear, descriptive title summarising the issue
  • Exact, reproducible steps to recreate the vulnerability
  • The specific assets, endpoints, or parameters affected
  • Evidence of exploitability — a working proof of concept, HTTP request/response, or screenshot
  • A concise description of the potential business or security impact
  • The conditions required for exploitation (authentication state, permissions, etc.)
  • Any relevant technical context such as server headers or error messages

Reports that are vague, non-reproducible, or rely solely on automated scanner output without manual validation may be deprioritised or classified as informational.
